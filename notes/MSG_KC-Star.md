Dear Mr. Donovan,

I'm a Kansas City writer with a personal account of working the graveyard shift at the Nelson Gallery-Atkins Museum during the landmark 1975 Exhibition of Archaeological Finds of the People's Republic of China.

The piece — "My Nights at the Museum" — covers the seven-week engagement from the perspective of a night-shift worker with unusual access: alone in the galleries at 2 a.m. with ancient bronzes and a loop of Chinese bells playing in an empty museum. It touches on the historical moment in US-China relations, the transformation of the Nelson Gallery for the exhibition, and what it meant to live two blocks from something the whole city was turning out for. Last year marked the 50th anniversary of the exhibition — a moment that still feels worth revisiting.

I understand the piece runs longer than your standard op-ed guidelines. I'd welcome the chance to discuss whether this fits your section — either at full length or in a condensed form.

The essay is available for review at: https://juren53.github.io/Chinese-Exhibit/
