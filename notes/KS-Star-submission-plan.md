# Kansas City Star — Submission Plan

*Research compiled: 2026-03-30*

---

## Overview

"My Nights at the Museum" is a strong candidate for KC Star publication. The piece has local Kansas City significance, a 50-year historical perspective, and a personal narrative angle — exactly what their opinion/community section says it looks for. The main challenge is word count: the essay as written is considerably longer than their 600–800 word op-ed guideline, so the submission strategy needs to account for that.

---

## Best Fit: Op-Ed / Guest Column (Opinion Section)

The Star no longer has a Sunday magazine. The realistic path is their **opinion/community voices section**. The editors actively seek first-person community perspectives and have stated they welcome local history and varied voices.

### Submission contacts

- **Primary email:** `oped@kcstar.com`
- **Opinion Editor:** Derek Donovan — `ddonovan@kcstar.com`

### What to include

- Short cover note (2–3 sentences on the piece and its angle)
- One-sentence author bio
- City of residence
- Daytime phone number
- Author photo (color)

### Guidelines

- **Word count:** 600–800 words (aim for ~700)
- Format: plain text or standard document
- Note: the Star edits submissions; accepted pieces may become property of the paper

---

## The Word Count Problem

The essay at current length far exceeds the op-ed limit. Two options:

1. **Condense a version** — write a ~700-word adaptation for the Star specifically, preserving the key moments (the gardening interview, the dry-brush technique, the Galloping Horse alone at 2 a.m.)
2. **Pitch first, ask about length** — email a brief pitch to Derek Donovan and ask whether they'd consider a longer-form submission or want a condensed version. This is the lower-risk approach.

**Recommended:** pitch first. If they're interested, they may have more flexibility than the published guidelines suggest, or they may assign an editor to work with you on a condensed version.

---

## Pitch language (suggested)

> I'm a Kansas City writer with a personal account of working the graveyard shift at the Nelson Gallery-Atkins Museum during the landmark 1975 Exhibition of Archaeological Finds of the People's Republic of China. The piece — "My Nights at the Museum" — covers the seven-week engagement from the perspective of a night-shift worker with unusual access: alone in the galleries at 2 a.m. with ancient bronzes and a loop of Chinese bells playing in an empty museum. It touches on the historical moment in US-China relations, the transformation of the Nelson Gallery for the Exhibit, and what it meant to live two blocks from something the whole city was turning out for. I'd welcome the chance to discuss whether this fits your section — either at full length or in a condensed form.
